# Jungle Tag

A Gorilla Tag–inspired jungle tag game for **Windows PC with keyboard and mouse**, made in **Godot 4.3** (free and open source).
Run, knuckle-sprint, climb giant trees, swing from vines, camouflage into the leaves, and tag your friends.

Everything in the game (the map, the gorilla characters, animations, sounds and music) is generated by the project's own code, so there are no third-party assets to license.

---

## 1. Running the game on Windows

1. Download **Godot 4.3 (Standard, not .NET)** for Windows from <https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.3-stable/>.
   It is a single `.exe` with no installer. Unzip it anywhere.
2. Unzip `jungle-tag.zip` to a folder, for example `C:\Games\jungle-tag`.
3. Start `Godot_v4.3-stable_win64.exe`. In the Project Manager click **Import**, browse to the folder and pick `project.godot`, then click **Import & Edit**.
4. The first time it opens, Godot imports the sounds (a few seconds). Then press **F5** (or the ▶ Play button, top right) to play.

> Newer Godot 4.x versions (4.4, 4.5) also open the project. Godot may offer to upgrade the project; click OK.

### Quick test checklist

| Feature | How to test |
|---|---|
| Movement | `W A S D`, mouse look, `Shift` sprint, `Space` jump, `Ctrl` crouch. Sprint then jump to see momentum carry. |
| Climbing | Walk up to a big tree, look at the trunk and **hold Left or Right mouse**. `W/S` climb up/down, `A/D` circle the trunk. **Alternate left and right clicks** to pull yourself up faster. `Space` = wall-jump off toward where you look. Let go of both buttons (or `Ctrl`) to drop. |
| Branches | Look up at a branch from underneath and hold a mouse button to **hang**. Move along it with `WASD`, press `Space` to pull up on top. You can also simply land on branches and walk along them. |
| Vines | Jump toward a hanging vine and press **E**. Use `W/A/S/D` to pump the swing and build speed. Press **E** or **Space** to let go and launch (Space adds a hop). Chain from vine to vine. |
| Camouflage | Press **G**. Your gorilla fades into a green/brown leaf pattern (with a leaf burst and sound). The bottom-right meter shows time left (15 s max) and then the 20 s cooldown. Moving fast lowers the “% hidden” number. Press G again to end it early. |
| Hiding | Bushes, tree canopies, the cave (west side), the secret tunnel out of the back of the cave, the hollow log, treehouses, and the ledge **behind the waterfall** at the north cliff. |
| HUD | Name, mode, gorilla count (top-left), round timer (top), role badge (top-right), camo meter (bottom-right). Hold **Tab** for the scoreboard. |
| Bananas | Eat 10, then press **F** for 30 seconds of double sprint speed. |
| Camera | **V** switches between third person and first person. Hold **B** for **binoculars** and scroll the mouse wheel to zoom in or out. |
| Pause | **Esc** (solo games actually pause). Settings are in the pause menu too. |

## 2. Main menu

* **Play** starts a solo match immediately. AI gorillas fill the empty slots so you can play alone.
* **Host Game** opens an online lobby (optionally a **private room** with a password).
* **Join Game** joins a friend with their **room code** (or an IP address).
* **Player Count** cycles 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16 players.
* **Settings**: graphics preset (Low/Medium/High/Ultra), fullscreen, V-Sync, FPS cap, render scale, view distance, vegetation density, shadows, ambient occlusion, glow, FPS counter, mouse sensitivity, invert Y, FOV and four volume sliders.
* **Quit**.

The right side of the menu is the **Match Setup**: your username, your gorilla colour (with a 3D preview), game mode, round time, player count, number of AI gorillas or “fill empty slots with AI”.

## 3. Game modes

* **Classic Tag**: one random gorilla starts as IT (or more, see **Taggers** below). Touching someone makes them IT (they're stunned for 2 s). **No tag-backs**: the new IT can't tag the gorilla who just tagged them, they have to get someone else. When time runs out, whoever spent the **least time as IT** wins.
* **Infection**: one gorilla (two with 10+ players) starts infected and turns lava-orange. Everyone they tag is infected too. The **last uninfected gorilla wins**. If time runs out, all survivors win.
* **Jungle Hide and Seek**: about one seeker per six players. Seekers are blindfolded for 25 s while hiders hide. Found hiders become seekers. Hiders win if anyone is still hidden when time runs out.

### Black holes, the maze and other surprises

* **Black holes** burst out of the ground 60 seconds into a round and then every 15 to 25 seconds, right in front of every player. Look for the tall purple beam and the "BLACK HOLE" marker with its distance. Get within about 11 m and they start sucking you in. Fall in and you land in a **dark underground maze**.
* Find the glowing **green EXIT** to climb back up into the jungle. **The Shadow**, a tagger who lives in the maze, hunts you down there. If it catches you, you go back to the start of the maze. It walks, so sprinting gets you away.
* **Power-ups** float around the jungle: gold **SPEED**, blue **SUPER JUMP**, green **CAMO REFILL**. Run through one to grab it.
* **FRENZY**: in the last 30 seconds of every round everyone moves faster.
* **Bananas**: 45 bananas are scattered around the jungle (a few are hidden deep in the waterfall cave). Walk into one to eat it, and it grows back somewhere else. The counter sits above the camo meter. When you've eaten **10**, press **F** for **2x sprint speed for 30 seconds**. A big countdown appears at the bottom of the screen.
* **Waterfall cave**: walk along the ledge behind the waterfall and through the doorway in the rock. Inside there's a glowing crystal grotto, a big crystal chamber and a maze of torch-lit tunnels with dead ends. Two hidden tunnels come out of the plateau's cliff sides (behind bushes), and a ladder shaft climbs up to the plateau top.
* **Weather**: every match starts in light rain under grey clouds (with the odd thunderclap). Every 3 minutes the weather flips between rain and sunshine.

**Black holes** (match setup): untick it to play without black holes, the maze and the Shadow.

**Taggers** (match setup): "Automatic" keeps each mode's usual number of taggers, or pick 1 to 4 taggers at the start of every round.

* **Black Hole Maze Run**: everyone (players and AI) starts inside the black hole maze. The Shadow hunts you. Each time you reach the green EXIT you score an escape and drop back in. Most escapes when time runs out wins.

**Chat**: press **Enter** (or **T**), type, and press Enter to send (Esc cancels). Messages show bottom-left and in a bubble over your gorilla. The nearest AI gorilla always answers you (or the one you name), and the AI gorillas chat with each other too.

Tagging is a touch (about 1.75 m). Clicking a mouse button when you are not next to anything climbable does a **swipe** that extends your reach briefly. Rounds restart automatically after a 9 s results screen. The host can restart the round or go back to the lobby from the pause menu.

## 4. Playing online with friends

The host's PC is the **authoritative server**. It decides tags, roles, the round timer and camouflage timing, and it runs the AI gorillas. Each player's own movement is simulated on their PC for responsiveness and streamed 20 times a second. The host sends everyone snapshots back, and other gorillas are smoothly interpolated.

1. The host clicks **Host Game** → **Open Lobby**. The lobby shows a **LAN room code** (for people on the same Wi-Fi) and a **Get Internet Code** button (looks up your public IP and copies the code).
2. Friends click **Join Game**, paste the code (and password for private rooms) and click **Join**.
3. Everyone can change name and colour in the lobby. The host picks mode, round time, player count and AI, then clicks **Start Match**.

**Internet play:** room codes encode the host's IP address and port. There is no central relay server, so for friends outside your home network you need **one** of:

* Forward **UDP port 24680** on your router to the host PC (search “port forwarding” + your router model), **or**
* Everyone installs a free LAN-over-internet VPN such as **Tailscale**, **ZeroTier** or **Radmin VPN**, joins the same network, and the host shares the LAN code / VPN IP.

Allow Godot (or `JungleTag.exe`) through **Windows Firewall** when prompted, on private networks.

## Automatic updates

`JungleTag.exe` checks this GitHub repository every time it starts. If there is a newer build, it shows "Downloading update...", downloads it (about 5 MB), and restarts into the new version. You never need to download a new zip. If you're offline, it just starts the version you already have. The build number is shown at the bottom of the main menu.

How it works: the `updates` branch holds `version.json` (`{"build": N}`) and `game.pck`. To publish, bump `config/build` in `project.godot` and run `GODOT=/path/to/godot tools/publish_update.sh`. Updates can change anything in the game but must stay on Godot 4.3, the engine version inside the exe.

## 5. Exporting a Windows .exe

1. In Godot: **Editor → Manage Export Templates → Download and Install** (one-time, about 1 GB, matches your Godot version).
2. **Project → Export…** The preset **“Windows Desktop”** is already set up in `export_presets.cfg` and writes `build/JungleTag.exe`.
   (If it's missing, click **Add… → Windows Desktop**.)
3. Click **Export Project**, choose the `build` folder, and leave **Export With Debug** unticked for a release build.
4. You get `JungleTag.exe` (the game data is embedded in the exe). Zip it and send it to friends. Everyone needs the **same build** to play together (the game checks a protocol version).

Optional: install `rcedit` and set its path in **Editor Settings → Export → Windows** if you want a custom exe icon. Windows SmartScreen may warn about an unsigned exe; click “More info → Run anyway”.

## 7. Performance notes

* Trees, branches, leaves, rocks, planks and plants are drawn with **MultiMesh** batches; ground plants are split into 50 m chunks with distance culling.
* Collision is simple primitives (heightmap terrain, cylinders, boxes, spheres) in a few static bodies; gorillas only collide with the world, not each other.
* Vines are bent in the vertex shader, so swinging costs almost nothing on the CPU.
* Use **Settings → Quality preset**. Low turns off shadows/SSAO/glow, lowers render scale and plant density.

## 8. Known limitations / ideas for later

* No public matchmaking server: online play is direct-connect (port forward or VPN). A relay (e.g. Steam networking, a WebRTC signalling server or Godot's `ENet` behind a VPS) could be added later.
* Players can't join a match that's already running; they join at the lobby (the host can return to the lobby at any time).
* Movement is client-simulated (positions are range-checked by the host, but not fully anti-cheat).
* AI uses steering + raycasts rather than a navmesh, so bots sometimes take silly routes or get briefly stuck. They don't climb into canopies, but they do climb trunks and ladders, swing on vines, hide in bushes/caves/treehouses and use camouflage.
* Key rebinding UI is not included (keys are defined in `scripts/autoload/settings.gd`).
